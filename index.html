Why Stone Breaker

  • Inhibits calcium oxalate crystal formation — the primary mechanism behind most kidney stones
  • Lignans (phyllanthin & hypophyllanthin) protect liver cells against toxin-induced damage
  • Antiviral research — most studied against Hepatitis B; promising results in early trials
  • Antidiabetic potential — extracts shown to lower fasting blood sugar and reduce pancreatic oxidative stress
  • Anti-inflammatory action comparable to ibuprofen in rat models, without synthetic side effects
  • Supports gallbladder — reduces bile sludge and may dissolve small gallstones over time
The antilithogenic properties of Phyllanthus niruri are evidenced by its ability to inhibit crystal formation and aggregation at multiple stages of kidney stone development. Bioactive lignans, flavonoids, and tannins identified across phytochemical studies are the primary responsible compounds. ↳ Journal of Nephrology Research · Ayurveda, Siddha & Unani Pharmacopoeia

Muthiga

Warburgia ugandensis · Pepperbark Tree

Kenya's own "natural pharmacy tree." The bark bites with heat — a signal of the drimane sesquiterpenes inside that have made this forest tree a cornerstone of East African traditional medicine for generations.

✦ Kikuyu Traditional ✦ Kenya / Uganda ✦ KEMRI Studied
15+Diseases treated
Anti-Microbial + Fungal
Trolox≈ Antioxidant level

Why Warburgia

  • Drimane sesquiterpenes — the signature compound class — deliver broad-spectrum antimicrobial and antifungal action
  • Antioxidant activity nearly equivalent to Trolox, a pharmaceutical antioxidant benchmark
  • Leaf tea is the primary East African treatment for malaria, fever, and bronchial infection
  • Bark chewed or infused for stomach ulcers, toothache, constipation, and rheumatic joint pain
  • Used in Kenyan ethnomedicine for coughs, respiratory disease, skin irritation, and hernia
  • Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI) studies confirm significant antimicrobial activity
The high therapeutic value of Warburgia ugandensis is attributed to the abundance of drimane sesquiterpenes in its stem bark and leaves. These compounds have also been shown to have potent antibacterial and antifungal activities in peer-reviewed studies conducted at KEMRI, Nairobi. ↳ Journal of Medicinal Plants Research · KEMRI · Academic Journals
⚠ Dosage matters: Warburgia is a potent herb. Excess bark extract has demonstrated cytotoxicity in cell models. Use as a tea or tincture at traditional proportions — a small piece of bark or a few leaves per cup. Not for prolonged high-dose use. Consult a practitioner if using alongside pharmaceuticals.

The weed the whole world overlooks. Every part edible, every part medicinal. Root for the liver; leaf for the kidneys. One of the most inulin-rich, bitterest, most liver-protective plants on earth.

✦ European Folk ✦ Chinese Medicine ✦ Global Tradition
25%Root inulin

Why Dandelion

  • Taraxasterol — the primary bioactive — modulates inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways, protecting liver cells from damage
  • Root contains up to 25% inulin — a prebiotic that feeds beneficial gut bacteria and stabilises blood sugar
  • Stimulates bile production and supports liver detoxification; particularly effective for liver congestion and inflammation
  • Leaf acts as a natural diuretic, flushing excess fluid and metabolic waste — uniquely replenishes potassium other diuretics deplete
  • Sesquiterpene lactones (taraxinic acid) responsible for the bitter taste that triggers digestive enzyme cascade
  • Preclinical studies show protection against chemically induced liver injury from alcohol, paracetamol, and carbon tetrachloride
Among the most relevant bioactive compounds of Taraxacum officinale is taraxasterol, which modulates inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways, helping to prevent liver damage. The phenolic compounds and flavonoids exhibit potent antioxidant activity, protecting hepatic tissue against free-radical damage. ↳ PubMed / NCBI · British Pharmacopoeia · Bulletin of WHO

Why Chayote

  • Vitexin, isovitexin, and orientin — flavonoids that together deliver antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antidiabetic action
  • Aqueous leaf extracts shown to reduce urea and creatinine in animal models — markers of improved kidney function
  • Anti-cardiovascular properties: cucurbitacins and phenolic compounds support healthy blood pressure and lipid profiles
  • Hypoglycemic effects confirmed in meta-analysis — supports glucose metabolism in Type 2 diabetes management
  • Hepatoprotective: extracts protect against chemically induced liver damage in animal trials
  • No neurotoxic or genotoxic metabolites detected in toxicological studies — safe for daily dietary use
Sechium edule extracts, including those from the fruit, leaves, and seeds, afford a protective effect against chemically induced liver damage while also ameliorating kidney injury by reducing serum urea and creatinine. Researchers identified 26 phenolic compounds in the fruit — classified as phenolic acids, flavonoids, and other polar compounds. ↳ Nature Research Intelligence · ScienceDirect · PubMed / NCBI · Molecules Journal
