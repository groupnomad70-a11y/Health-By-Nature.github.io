Muthiga

Warburgia ugandensis · Pepperbark Tree

Kenya's own "natural pharmacy tree." The bark bites with heat — a signal of the drimane sesquiterpenes inside that have made this forest tree a cornerstone of East African traditional medicine for generations.

✦ Kikuyu Traditional ✦ Kenya / Uganda ✦ KEMRI Studied
15+ Diseases treated
Anti- Microbial + Fungal
Trolox ≈ Antioxidant level

Why Warburgia

  • Drimane sesquiterpenes — the signature compound class — deliver broad-spectrum antimicrobial and antifungal action
  • Antioxidant activity nearly equivalent to Trolox, a pharmaceutical antioxidant benchmark
  • Leaf tea is the primary East African treatment for malaria, fever, and bronchial infection
  • Bark chewed or infused for stomach ulcers, toothache, constipation, and rheumatic joint pain
  • Used in Kenyan ethnomedicine for coughs, respiratory disease, skin irritation, and hernia
  • Kenya Medical Research Institute (KEMRI) studies confirm significant antimicrobial activity

The high therapeutic value of Warburgia ugandensis is attributed to the abundance of drimane sesquiterpenes in its stem bark and leaves. These compounds have also been shown to have potent antibacterial and antifungal activities in peer-reviewed studies conducted at KEMRI, Nairobi.

↳ Journal of Medicinal Plants Research · KEMRI · Academic Journals
⚠ Dosage matters: Warburgia is a potent herb. Excess bark extract has demonstrated cytotoxicity in cell models. Dandelion

Taraxacum officinale

The weed the whole world overlooks. Every part edible, every part medicinal. Root for the liver; leaf for the kidneys. One of the most inulin-rich, bitterest, most liver-protective plants on earth.

✦ European Folk ✦ Chinese Medicine ✦ Global Tradition
25% Root inulin
Liver Bile stimulant
K+ Potassium-sparing

Why Dandelion

  • Taraxasterol — the primary bioactive — modulates inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways, protecting liver cells from damage
  • Root contains up to 25% inulin — a prebiotic that feeds beneficial gut bacteria and stabilises blood sugar
  • Stimulates bile production and supports liver detoxification; particularly effective for liver congestion and inflammation
  • Leaf acts as a natural diuretic, flushing excess fluid and metabolic waste — uniquely replenishes potassium other diuretics deplete
  • Sesquiterpene lactones (taraxinic acid) responsible for the bitter taste that triggers digestive enzyme cascade
  • Preclinical studies show protection against chemically induced liver injury from alcohol, paracetamol, and carbon tetrachloride

Among the most relevant bioactive compounds of Taraxacum officinale is taraxasterol, which modulates inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways, helping to prevent liver damage. The phenolic compounds and flavonoids exhibit potent antioxidant activity, protecting hepatic tissue against free-radical damage.
