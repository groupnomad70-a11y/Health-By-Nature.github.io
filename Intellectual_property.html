Internet Governance & IP Policy

Policy work rooted in two continents.

A two-node structure spanning Germany and Kenya — bridging ICANN/WIPO institutions with KENIC/KIPI community governance to protect origin, domain rights, and traditional knowledge.

Architecture

One framework, two jurisdictions.

The Gibbs Empowerment Framework operates a two-node governance structure that connects institutional capacity in Germany with community rootedness in Kenya. Neither node works in isolation — the value is in the bridge.

The German node provides access to European IP law, ICANN participation channels, and donor-legible institutional interfaces (GIZ, BMZ, Erasmus+). The Kenyan node grounds that access in the farming community, school graduates, and cooperative structures at Kavutiri.

Domain name policy, GI certification, and traditional knowledge advocacy are each approached through this dual lens: what does the institution require, and what does the community actually need?

German Node · Hesse, DE

DENIC → ICANN → WIPO

Entry point for European domain name governance, ICANN policy participation, and WIPO arbitration procedures. Maintains access to UDRP/URS complaint mechanisms and monitors gTLD policy cycles relevant to agricultural and geographic names.

DENIC → ICANN → WIPO → IGC
Kenyan Node · Embu, KE

KENIC → KIPI → ARIPO

Community-facing node managing .ke ccTLD engagement, GI applications via Kenya Industrial Property Institute, and regional IP coordination through ARIPO. Anchored in Kavutiri Farm's documented soil, elevation, and processing practices — the evidentiary base for any GI claim.

KENIC → KIPI → ARIPO
Work areas

Active policy tracks

Four parallel tracks, each with a distinct institutional interface and a distinct community outcome.

Track 01

Domain name arbitration & UDRP

Active

ICANN UDRP / URS procedures

Domain name disputes involving geographic, agricultural, or community-owned terms are processed through ICANN's Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy (UDRP) and the newer Uniform Rapid Suspension (URS) system. This track monitors and participates in proceedings relevant to Kenyan coffee origin names, cascara terminology, and the Kavutiri geographic identifier.

Youth participants in Tier 2 of the Empowerment Framework receive structured training in domain registration best practice, dispute prevention, and basic UDRP literacy — converting what is ordinarily an institutional-only process into a community skill.

ICANN UDRP URS WIPO D-Center DENIC
Track 02

Geographic Indication certification

In progress

Kavutiri / Embu GI dossier — KIPI pathway

Kavutiri Farm sits on polygon KE162 (KENSOTERv2): Nitisol NTu soil classification, 1,400–1,600 m above sea level, Land Evaluation Index 72/100. This soil-elevation-variety combination is the evidentiary core of a GI claim linking the Embu highland terroir to a demonstrably distinct cup profile.

The application pathway runs through Kenya Industrial Property Institute (KIPI) and, once granted, pursues equivalence recognition under EU Regulation 2024/1143 (the successor framework to 2081/92). Parallel work tracks the WIPO Lisbon System as a second-channel international filing route.

KIPI EU Reg 2024/1143 Lisbon System KENSOTERv2 Nitisol NTu
Track 03

Traditional Knowledge labels & TCE

Scoping

Local Contexts / WIPO IGC interface

Traditional Knowledge (TK) Labels, issued through the Local Contexts platform, provide community-controlled attribution for practices whose origins predate formal IP systems. For Kavutiri, candidate claims include the honey processing lineage, cascara preparation as a highland beverage tradition, and any documented sylvotherapy practices rooted in Meru or Kikuyu forest knowledge.

This track monitors WIPO Intergovernmental Committee (IGC) sessions on genetic resources, traditional knowledge, and folklore — the multilateral negotiation space where TK Labels are increasingly cited as community-led IP instruments. Outputs feed into the GI dossier as supporting cultural provenance evidence.

Local Contexts WIPO IGC CBD / Nagoya TCE
Track 04

Youth domain & IP literacy pipeline

Active

Gibbs Empowerment Framework — Tier 2

The second tier of the Gibbs Empowerment Framework converts institutional processes into teachable skills for Kenyan high school graduates. Participants learn domain registration mechanics, basic WHOIS interpretation, UDRP dispute prevention, and the difference between a trademark, a GI, and a TK label — grounding abstract policy in decisions they will actually face as small business owners.

The German node provides curriculum anchoring through European IP law comparatives; the Kenyan node provides the practical context. Outcomes are designed to be legible to GIZ, BMZ, and Erasmus+ funding frameworks.

GEF Tier 2 KENIC GIZ / BMZ Erasmus+
Institutional pathway

From soil data to protected origin.

The GI certification process is not a single filing — it is a documented chain from physical evidence to international recognition. Each step builds on the last, and each step creates a public record that strengthens the community's position.

The two-node structure means that at every institutional interface, there is a present interlocutor: someone in Germany who understands European frameworks, and someone in Kenya who can verify the ground truth.

01

Soil & terroir documentation

KENSOTERv2 polygon KE162 — Nitisol NTu, 1,400–1,600 m, LEI 72/100. QGIS-based boundary delimitation of the Kavutiri growing zone within Embu County.

KENSOTERv2 QGIS ALR 6114
02

Cup profile & process link

Establishing the demonstrable connection between soil/elevation and the honey-processed cup character — the legal requirement under GI frameworks that distinguishes a true GI from a mere geographic name.

Honey Process SCA Protocol
03

KIPI application

Formal GI application to Kenya Industrial Property Institute. Requires the product specification, geographic delimitation, proof of origin-quality link, and evidence of the controlling community structure (cooperative, producer group, or equivalent).

KIPI KE GI Act
04

EU equivalence & Lisbon filing

Post-grant, pursuing equivalence recognition under EU Regulation 2024/1143 and parallel filing via the WIPO Lisbon System for international protection across Lisbon Agreement member states.

EU 2024/1143 Lisbon System WIPO
05

TK label overlay

Once GI is secured, TK Labels from Local Contexts provide a complementary community-controlled layer — signalling not just where the coffee comes from, but who holds governance rights over how that knowledge is shared and used.

Local Contexts WIPO IGC TK Attribution
